1. Product purpose and language
==============================
PACE is a sport-dating and active-lifestyle connection product. It helps people
with shared sports interests meet an activity partner, a potential romantic
partner, or someone who could become both. Sport provides a shared lifestyle and
opportunities to spend time together. PACE is not a performance leaderboard or
fitness-program manager, and meeting someone does not have to lead to a date.

Core journey: authentic profile → discover suitable people → genuine mutual likes
→ match → chat → arrange an activity or meet in person. Plus can offer structured
invitations before matching.
Supporting journeys: connected users share moments, and nearby public activities
create natural opportunities to meet and spend time together.

Content and copy requirements:
- Emphasize meeting people, interests, connection, time together, and shared plans.
  Keep the tone concise, natural, and mature.
- Avoid making every screen sound like training software through repetitive
  references to performance, competition, intensity, or training assignments.
- Avoid excessive flirtation, pressure to date, or guaranteed-match promises.
  People looking for an activity partner are also part of the intended audience.
- Use familiar labels: Matches, Chat, Profile, and Settings & account.
- Persist stable IDs; Chinese and English names are presentation labels only.
  See CONTEXT.md for terminology.
- Current dictionaries include Chinese, English, French, Spanish, and German;
  this does not mean every flow is fully localized.
- Maintain project documentation in English. Documentation language does not
  change the app's localization requirements or the user's conversation language.

2. Established product rules
============================
2.1 Discovery and matching
- Distance filtering is a basic feature, one tap away from Discovery; it must
  remain available without a subscription.
- If nobody meets the distance/preferences, show an honest empty state and let
  the user change the filters. Do not silently expand the radius, add fake people,
  or present a waitlist action without a real supporting service.
- Likes, matches, unread counts, and incoming-like counts must reflect real
  records. A one-sided like cannot be presented as a match.
- A normal match requires genuine mutual likes. Whether accepting an unmatched
  invitation creates a relationship remains a product decision.
- Sports ability describes lifestyle, not a person's worth. The experience should
  not rely solely on appearance or athletic performance.

2.2 Free and Plus
- Matching, chatting with matches, invitations within a match, distance filtering,
  and browsing/joining public activities are free.
- Viewing full profiles of people who liked you, directly inviting an unmatched
  person, and creating public activities require Plus.
- Creating a public activity also requires trusted host verification. The host
  counts toward capacity and cannot simply leave their own activity; cancellation
  needs a separate flow.
- Sending an invitation creates only a pending invitation. It must not create a
  match or conversation automatically or imply that the recipient accepted.
- Every submission must recheck the active subscription, match relationship,
  blocks in either direction, user/activity status, and quotas. A check when the
  form opens cannot replace server authorization at submission time.
- Plus cannot bypass reporting, bans, blocks, harassment prevention, capacity,
  or privacy restrictions.
- Canceling renewal retains benefits for the paid period. Expiration or revocation
  prevents new actions that require membership.
- Billing is a top-level settings entry. Offer purchase restoration and system
  subscription management without retention obstacles. Closing Apple's management
  sheet is not proof of cancellation; rely on trusted transaction state.
- Prices, currencies, and billing periods come from real StoreKit product
  configuration. The UI must not invent prices or successful payments.
- The local preview defaults to free. Purchase/restore are unavailable without
  a purchase bridge; localStorage cannot grant Plus.
- The product specification requires marketing to stop after subscription
  cancellation or account deletion, based on server-confirmed state. Separate
  transactional notifications from marketing, which requires explicit opt-in.

2.3 Trust and profiles
- Allow up to six profile photos; the first leads the profile. Preserve sensible
  face cropping and focal positioning.
- Height and ethnicity have optional public visibility. Public APIs must omit
  values the user has hidden, not merely hide them in the UI.
- Exact birth dates, home addresses, and activity routes are not public profile
  data. Public distance must be appropriately coarsened.
- Selfie/liveness verification is mandatory in the earlier specification but is
  not truly implemented. Never present a demo badge as completed verification.
  Production eligibility rules, provider selection, and failure handling require
  confirmation and integration.
- Strava / Apple Health / Garmin are optional integration directions, not
  currently connected services.
- The earlier specification has no minimum bio length or maximum sports count.
  Current onboarding imposes conflicting constraints; these are unresolved
  implementation differences, not approved new product rules.
- Moments are intended for connected people. Selecting an author's avatar/name
  opens that person's existing Profile view.
- Account deletion must revoke sessions, stop discovery/marketing, and process
  data deletion. A toast alone is not account deletion.

4. Current implementation and launch blockers
============================================
4.1 Reusable foundations already present
- HTML/CSS/native ES Modules, feature-level files, local fonts, and main images.
- Local/HTTP repository boundaries, a shared API client, pure logic, and contract
  tests.
- Onboarding, multilingual copy, session-restoration loading, Discovery filters
  and gallery, match/chat screens, profile editing, Moments, Plus screens, and
  activity lists/filtering/details/local join/leave interactions.
- An in-memory reference service for Plus/activity/Apple transaction rules and
  a StoreKit bridge interface. These are rules and integration boundaries, not
  deployed services.

4.2 Gaps that must remain explicit
Full evidence is in the two 2026-09-26 audits.
- Registration passwords do not create real accounts. Google/Apple entries use
  demo identities. Real token exchange, email verification/recovery, secure account
  linking, and a complete session lifecycle are missing.
- Local remembered login stores only a versioned, 30-day demo marker, not an
  account, profile, or membership. Resuming the local app does not prove production
  authentication or cross-device profile persistence.
- Discovery responses mainly control visibility while the card remains fixed to
  Maya. Like demonstrates a match without real mutual likes or a database
  transaction creating a unique match/conversation.
- Chat displays sample messages; sending appends only to the DOM and is lost when
  reopened. There is no real conversation history or realtime service.
- The in-chat invitation form only closes and reports success; it persists neither
  recipient nor invitation. Discovery's direct invite has a repository boundary,
  but the two paths are not yet a complete, unified domain service.
- Profile startup does not fully load the user's saved profile, and some
  onboarding fields are not fully persisted.
- Profile/Moments photos use local blob previews. The media-upload API boundary
  does not complete production uploading. Moments contain fixed-author and
  client-controlled verification data; derive identity from the session and
  validate it on the server.
- Sample person IDs differ between maya and user_maya. A missing verified field
  can default to showing a badge.
- "Selfie verified" and "Garmin connected" are demo states, not real results.
- Account deletion only sets local state and shows a notification; it does not
  revoke sessions or actually clean up data.
- Reporting, blocks in both directions, unmatching, moderation operations, and
  real message/invitation notifications are missing.
- There is no native iOS target, real StoreKit adapter, Apple JWS verifier,
  webhook, persistent subscription ledger, or APNs configuration. App Store
  integration cannot currently be described as complete.
- Global HTTP timeout/401 handling, error retries, public-DTO privacy, and media
  validation still need work.
- Onboarding and some forms/system messages still mix English with other selected
  languages. Tab keyboard semantics, match-dialog focus, upload limits, and
  canceled-upload cleanup have gaps. A full-app audit does not mean these are fixed.

Critical test boundaries:
- Unauthenticated requests or forged actors cannot read/write protected data.
  Guessing an ID must not expose someone else's private chat or hidden profile.
- A one-sided Like creates no match; concurrent mutual likes create exactly one.
  Multiple devices cannot bypass quotas.
- A free user's unmatched invitation is rejected with no writes. Invitations
  within a match remain free. Membership expiring after form opening still causes
  rejection at submission.
- Retries of the same message/invitation/transaction are idempotent. Concurrent
  acceptance and cancellation produce only one legal outcome.
- When several users request the last place, only one succeeds; capacity is never
  exceeded.
- Forged, wrong-account, or wrong-environment Apple transactions cannot activate
  membership. Refunds and stale webhooks must not revive entitlements.
- Recheck blocks, bans, and deletion at final submission. Caches, old links, and
  paid membership cannot bypass these checks.
- Test cross-account media attachment, corrupt files, disguised formats, limits,
  EXIF handling, and orphaned objects.
- Network loss, late responses, service restarts, time boundaries/DST, and backup
  restoration must not create data loss or duplication.

Suggested order after explicit backend authorization (a proposal, not an approved
technology selection): confirm release scope and state machines → contracts/schema/
isolated test database → failing integration tests first → identity/profile/media
foundations → real multi-user discovery/matching/chat/invitation journeys → chosen
extensions and payments → failure/performance/device/store-review acceptance.
Implement security and privacy with each module, rather than adding them last.
Backend database, concurrency, and HTTP authorization tests must exercise a real,
isolated service and database; in-memory reference tests cannot replace them.
For Apple, report verifier-fixture integration results and real Sandbox evidence
separately.

7. Architecture and code boundaries
==================================
Current stack: static HTML + CSS + native JavaScript ES Modules, without a
React/Vite build. Python serves local static HTTP only. Playwright is a development
test dependency, not a production service. Handoff is not a reason to change
frameworks or rewrite the app. Propose any migration separately with benefits and
a rollback strategy.

Module map:
index.html                 Initial screens, startup shell, static Discovery card,
                           adapter selection, and entry asset versions.
app.js                     Composition, navigation, Discovery interactions, and
                           localization; avoid accumulating more business logic.
features/onboarding        Registration/login UI, session restoration, startup gate.
features/launch_assets     First image/texture/font readiness and release after
                           failure or timeout.
features/account           Existing Me, edit/preview, settings/privacy/account-deletion UI.
features/chat              Chat/Profile and in-chat invitation UI; persistent
                           business operations still need integration.
features/moments           Connected feed, author navigation, comments/publishing UI.
features/membership        Plus, billing, unmatched direct invitations;
                           membership_copy contains its copy.
features/train             Public activities, categories/details/join/host UI;
                           activity_seed is demo data only.
features/storekit_bridge   Native purchase/restore/manage contract and validation
                           of bridge responses.
features/app_copy          Shared multilingual copy; dom / photo_presentation
                           are small presentation utilities.
pace_repository.mjs        Shared data interface with local and HTTP adapters.
api_client.mjs             /api/v1 paths, request parameters/JSON/errors/credentials.
app_logic / connection_feed / sport_catalog / engagement_service
                           Pure rules, catalog, and in-memory reference service;
                           no dependency on the DOM or browser networking.

Dependency direction: UI → repository → HTTP adapter/API client → future server.
Pure domain rules may be called by other modules; they must not depend on the UI.
Keep network requests out of individual features. Before adding a business field,
define its inputs, public outputs, role permissions, state transitions, failure
modes, and idempotency contract. Chinese display names are not primary keys.
Standardize user/profile/match/conversation IDs; the server determines the actor.
Use reproducible local seeds. HTTP failures must surface as real errors, never a
fallback to fake data. Keep matched and unmatched invitation paths consistent
rather than fixing one while leaving contradictory rules in the other.

Style cascade: styles.css owns fonts and explicit imports in this order:
base → discovery → activity → onboarding → conversation → train → membership.
Keep styles in the owning feature file. Avoid endless global overrides or higher
specificity as a default fix. Historical overrides exist: read the effective final
rules before editing. Separate cleanup from behavior changes and use screenshots
to check for regressions. Matching asset cache stamps must stay consistent across
HTML, all production imports, CSS, and relevant fixtures; contract tests enforce
this. Add new modules to package syntax checks and dependency constraints.

8. Security and data quality standards
=====================================
These are release acceptance requirements, not a statement of implementation.
- The server is authoritative for identity, membership, matches, verification,
  authorship, hosting, attendance counts, and public fields. Each mutation needs
  authentication, resource authorization, input-schema validation, and a final
  state check.
- Design Web cookie HttpOnly/Secure/SameSite settings, CSRF protection, expiry,
  and revocation together. Store native long-lived credentials in system secure
  storage. Passwords/tokens do not belong in localStorage.
- Render untrusted text with textContent or centralized escaping. Validate URL
  protocols and ownership separately. HTML escaping alone does not secure uploads,
  external links, or all script-injection paths.
- Use atomic transactions and database constraints for unique matches, message/
  transaction idempotency, capacity, and relationship permissions. Single-process
  memory locks or disabled buttons do not establish server concurrency correctness.
- Keep secrets in environment secret stores. Git may contain .env.example with
  empty/dummy values, never .env, Apple .p8 files, certificates/private keys,
  production databases, user media/chats, or logs containing tokens.
- Log request IDs, error codes, and necessary state; redact passwords, sessions,
  full JWS payloads, private chats, and precise locations. Return understandable,
  recoverable errors without internal stacks or account-enumeration information.
- Validate actual upload content, size, dimensions, count, and ownership; remove
  sensitive metadata and make moderation/cleanup traceable.
- Reporting, blocking, unmatching, operational contact, and a complete response
  workflow are basic safety capabilities for a dating product.
- Minimize collected data. Apply hidden-field rules consistently to every read
  path. Account deletion includes derived caches and asynchronous jobs.
- Review dependency sources/licenses and commit lockfile changes. Scan for
  vulnerabilities and assess findings individually instead of blindly forcing
  upgrades.
- Production requires separate environments, HTTPS, least privilege, monitoring/
  alerts, backup restoration drills, and rollback procedures.
- Platform policies change. After confirming target markets, recheck Apple's
  sign-in/subscription/privacy/UGC/account-deletion requirements:
  https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/
  Do not claim launch readiness before real purchases, device testing, TestFlight,
  store information, and operational acceptance are complete.

10. Collaboration, review, and completion criteria
================================================
These collaboration conventions were established for the handoff; GitHub branch
protection still needs repository configuration.
- Keep main as a runnable, reviewable baseline. Use a small, purpose-named branch
  per task.
- Agree on file ownership and interfaces before two developers/agents edit in
  parallel, especially app.js and global CSS. Inspect workspace changes before
  starting; preserve others' work instead of overwriting it with reset/clean.
- Each PR solves one clear problem. Separate refactoring, behavior changes, and
  visual redesign where practical.
- PR descriptions explain the user problem, final behavior, test evidence,
  interface/migration impact, and unresolved limitations.
- Merge shared-main changes through review. At least one peer review is
  recommended, with required checks passing before merge. Do not force-push shared
  history, delete/reupload the whole project as a shortcut, or commit node_modules.
- Follow the existing native-module conventions, two-space indentation, LF, and
  stable naming. Comments explain reasons and boundaries, not duplicate control
  flow. Prefer small, clear domain modules over layers of abstraction.
- UI handles user intent and pending/error/retry states. Optimistic updates must
  roll back on failure, and repeated submissions must be safe.
- A success message must correspond to a real committed result. Unimplemented
  actions need honest states, not fabricated success messages.
- Update tests and integration documents with type/schema/data-contract changes.
  Record major technology decisions with rationale, costs, compatibility, and
  rollback rather than leaving them only in a chat.
- Production deployment, paid purchases, store publication, inviting unknown
  collaborators, and changing repository visibility require authorization for
  that task. Kevin supplied the account kev1nwuu and allowed the repository name
  to be chosen. The target is private repository kev1nwuu/pace-sport-dating;
  GitHub is the authority for actual creation/push status.
- Write and maintain first-party project documentation in English, including
  handoffs, specifications, test plans, and agent instructions. Preserve technical
  identifiers and third-party license text. This does not remove app localization.

Definition of Done:
The requirement and boundaries are clear; relevant tests pass with honest evidence;
UI changes have been inspected; failure paths are recoverable; no new sensitive
material enters Git; authorization/idempotency/migration impact is assessed;
documentation is current; and review is complete. Finished code, a plausible-looking
button, or passing unit tests alone do not establish production readiness.

11. Running the project on a collaborator's computer
==================================================
Prerequisites: Git, Node.js 20+ (prefer one supported version shared by the team),
npm, and Python 3. Clone the repository provided by Kevin, enter its root, and run:
  npm ci
  npx playwright install chromium
  npm start
Open http://127.0.0.1:4173/ in a browser. Do not double-click file://index.html;
ES module loading and storage origins differ and may cause a blank page or
behavior inconsistent with the local server.

Run checks in another terminal:
  npm run check
  npm run test:unit
  npm run test:e2e
Complete verification: npm run verify
Intentional, reviewed visual-baseline updates: npm run test:e2e:update
If a Linux CI runner lacks browser system dependencies, install them using the
official Playwright procedure in an isolated runner. If port 4173 is occupied,
identify the running project before acting; do not arbitrarily stop someone
else's service.

The pace-data-adapter value in index.html defaults to local. The static Python
server does not provide /api/v1; changing the value to http cannot create a backend.
Switch only when real API authentication, contracts, and same-origin/proxy setup
are available. First-time visitors can use local demo sign-in. Do not enter real
passwords, identity documents, or private photos during demo testing. Real account
integration requires separate development/staging/production configuration;
never share production user data with those test environments.

13. Suggested first collaboration task after handoff
===================================================
The collaborator should first reproduce the local app and read the 18-module
backend backlog and its 62 acceptance cases. Together, resolve the first three
questions in section 6 and review the latest invitation icon. Then agree on the
initial release scope, division of work, and review process. Begin test-first
backend implementation only when Kevin gives the instruction. Before that,
reviewing tests, designing contracts, and fixing explicitly authorized frontend
issues are appropriate. Avoid implementing every extension at once and losing
control of the architecture.

Reusable task prompt for a new Codex agent:
"Read AGENTS.md and PROJECT_HANDOFF.txt first, then inspect the current code and
relevant API/test documents. Briefly explain your understanding of PACE's purpose,
confirmed rules, demo limitations, and the scope of this change in English.
Preserve the latest charcoal/lime design and free/Plus access boundaries. Begin
backend implementation or production publication only after Kevin explicitly
authorizes it; once approved, write failing acceptance tests first. Each delivery
must explain what changed, how it was verified, and what remains unverified.
Do not present demo success as proof of real multi-user, payment, or security flows."
