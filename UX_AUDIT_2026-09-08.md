## Journey audit

1. **Entry and returning login** — Welcome has a clear primary email action and secondary social choices. Log in is a dedicated page with labeled email/password fields, inline validation, loading feedback, and the requested automatic PACE launch transition. The underlying app is `aria-hidden` while onboarding is active.
2. **Discover and photos** — All four filters fit in one row at the mobile width; advanced preferences remain one tap away. Four local Maya photos advance correctly, contain no broken assets, and use face-safe thumbnail crops. The card, vertical sport rail, weekly activity statistic, decisions, and bottom navigation remain visible without horizontal overflow.
3. **No-results state** — A specific sport with no nearby candidate replaces the card with concise copy, suggests changing sport or distance, and exposes no nonfunctional waitlist CTA.
4. **Decision and connection** — Like opens a viewport-centred connection state. Both identity cards meet the intended large-card ratio, and `Start the conversation` enters a usable chat.
5. **Chat, profile, and invitation** — Message submission is disabled when empty and resets after send. The connected profile renders all four supplied photos. The training invitation stays vertically centred, contains its own scroll area when needed, validates required fields/date, and leaves the background inert.
6. **Matches** — The page uses the requested direct `Matches` title with no extra top-brand row. The full Maya row is the single chat target; there is no separate arrow or Chat button. The thumbnail keeps Maya's face visible.
7. **Community** — Only connected-member posts render. Like/unlike is reversible, zero comments reads `No comments`, the comment surface rises from the bottom and can be dismissed by its drag handle, and publishing stays disabled until required content is valid.
8. **Train** — The activity card and paid host-tool entry are readable and truthful. The paid entry opens a transparent Plus explanation rather than silently blocking the action.
9. **Profile and settings** — Avatar edit opens a full-screen editor, not a dialog. The mobile-native height picker converts `180 cm` to `5′ 11″` and back, and each detail owns its visibility switch. View shows only enabled values; Done persists through the repository seam. Settings swaps the lower panel in place, hides the profile overview, and exposes language, billing, privacy, and deletion at one level.
10. **Notifications and localization** — With no unread data, the notification dot is hidden and the response is a text-only pill. English and Simplified Chinese switch live and retain the same navigation/settings interface.

## General health

- **Hierarchy and visual balance:** Photo-first discovery, quieter Matches/settings surfaces, and sport-context details create the intended middle ground between a sports utility and a conventional dating app.
- **Mobile ergonomics:** Key targets are touch-sized; filters do not require horizontal scrolling; bottom sheets, full-screen editing, native selects, and persistent navigation behave as mobile patterns.
- **Accessibility baseline:** Labeled controls, alt text, modal focus/inert behavior, keyboard Escape behavior, reduced-motion handling, and baseline contrast/structure are covered by the browser suite.
- **Feedback and failures:** Primary submits communicate disabled/loading states. Repository failures show localized feedback and restore controls.
- **Consistency:** Feature JavaScript, repository/transport boundaries, local assets, five language interfaces, and feature-owned CSS are consistent. All production imports share one cache stamp.

## Fixes applied during this final pass

- Wired the PACE brand button to a real Home/Discover action.
- Replaced dead Garmin/Apple Health-looking buttons with truthful status rows while retaining the real selfie verification action.
- Added localized network-failure recovery to discovery, profile save, likes, comments, feed loading, and publishing.
- Added regression coverage for Home navigation and static privacy status rows.
- Serialized browser tests with a realistic timeout to eliminate local CPU-contention flakes.
- Unified every production JavaScript and CSS cache stamp to avoid mixed-version deployment.
- Updated the product specification so distance/filter access, the honest empty state, photo-first hierarchy, Community, Matches, and in-page settings match the accepted product decisions.

## Remaining boundaries

These are not frontend regressions: authentication, durable account/profile/feed data, server-owned mutual matches, realtime messages/notifications, media storage, fitness integrations, verification, billing, deletion/privacy workflows, moderation, analytics, monitoring, rollback, and security controls still require backend/infrastructure work. Keep the local adapter enabled until those contracts are implemented and exercised in a non-production environment.
